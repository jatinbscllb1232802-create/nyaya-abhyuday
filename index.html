About the Fest

The Moot Court Committee of the School of Law, Forensic Justice and Policy Studies, National Forensic Sciences University, presents Nyaya Abhyuday — a national techno-legal platform that brings together appellate advocacy on emerging technology law and rigorous trial advocacy training.

The previous edition drew more than 55 teams from National Law Universities and leading law colleges across India. The fest continues under the guidance of the Dean and the organising faculty with the objective of producing advocates capable of handling both complex constitutional-tech questions and the practical demands of the trial court.

Competitions

4th NFSU National Technological Moot Court Competition

Participants argue a complex proposition involving artificial intelligence, autonomous weapon systems, constitutional rights, and questions of accountability and state responsibility. The competition follows the traditional appellate moot format with memorials and oral rounds before a bench.

  • Team: 2 Speakers + 1 Researcher
  • Memorials required from both sides (Bluebook 21st Edition)
  • Rounds: Preliminary → Quarter-final → Semi-final → Final
  • Researcher’s Test conducted on Day 1 (scores count toward Preliminary ranking)
  • Oral submissions in English only

2nd NFSU National Trial Advocacy Competition

A full trial simulation designed to develop practical litigation skills. Teams conduct examination-in-chief, cross-examination, raise and argue objections, handle evidence, and deliver opening and closing statements under timed conditions.

  • Focus on courtroom craft and strategy
  • Witness examination and evidence management
  • Court etiquette and professional conduct scored
  • Designed to complement the appellate skills tested in the Technological Moot

Rules & Format

Each team shall consist of three students: two Speakers and one Researcher. The competition is open to students enrolled in 3-year or 5-year LL.B. programmes. Institution identity must not be disclosed at any stage of the competition.

Memorials must be prepared from both sides. Maximum length: 2,500 words of written arguments (inclusive of prayer). Font: Bookman Old Style. Citations: Harvard Bluebook 21st Edition. Soft copies (Word + PDF) and five hard copies from each side are required. Late submission attracts a penalty of 5 marks per day.

Preliminary Rounds: 50 minutes (25 minutes per team). Quarter-final & Semi-final: 1 hour 30 minutes (45 minutes per team). Final: 2 hours (1 hour per team). Sides and opposing teams are determined by draw of lots. Extension of time is at the sole discretion of the Bench.

Written Memorial (50 marks): Knowledge of facts & law (10), Articulate analysis (10), Extent & use of research (10), Format & citation (10), Grammar & style (10).

Oral Rounds (50 marks per speaker): Marshalling of facts (5), Articulation of issues (5), Presentation & communication (5), Response to questions (5), Application of legal principles (15), Use of authorities (10), Courtroom etiquette (5).

Researcher’s Test scores are taken into account for Preliminary Round ranking.

Strict court formals are mandatory. Male participants: white shirt, black trousers, black tie, black blazer, black shoes. Female participants: white salwar-kurta or white shirt with black trousers, black blazer, black shoes. Scouting is strictly prohibited and will lead to disqualification.
